\section{The question}

A market maker quotes a bid and an ask around a mid price, earns the spread when
someone trades against a quote, and carries the resulting inventory at risk. When
the world is simple enough, the optimal quotes are known in closed form. When it
is not, they are not.

This project builds a family of simulated worlds $\{\mathcal{M}_\theta\}$ with one
distinguished point $\thnull$ where the closed form is exactly optimal, and asks a
single question:

\begin{quote}
As the world moves away from $\thnull$ along one coefficient at a time, how much
does a reinforcement learning agent gain over the closed form, and which piece of
information buys that gain?
\end{quote}

The point of the design is that the answer is falsifiable. At $\thnull$ the agent
must \emph{recover} the closed form and beat nothing; anywhere else, a gain is
only credited when a blind agent of the same size and budget does not obtain it.

% ---------------------------------------------------------------
\section{The criterion}

Let $S_t$ be the mid price, $q_t$ the inventory in units, and $X_t$ the cash.
Write $W_t = X_t + q_t S_t$ for the mark-to-market wealth. Over one step of length
$\dt$ the reward is

\begin{equation}
  r_t \;=\; \Delta W_t \;-\; \phi\, q_t^2\, \dt ,
  \qquad
  \Delta W_t \;=\; \underbrace{\textstyle\sum \delta^{\pm}}_{\text{spread}}
  \;+\; \underbrace{q_t\,\Delta S_t}_{\text{carry}}
  \;+\; \underbrace{\Delta q_t\,\Delta S_t}_{\text{adverse}} ,
\end{equation}

where $\delta^{+}$ and $\delta^{-}$ are the half-spreads quoted on the ask and bid
side, and $\phi \ge 0$ prices inventory risk. The three-term decomposition is an
identity, not an approximation, and the simulator asserts it at every step to
$10^{-9}$: it is what makes ``the agent earned more spread'' and ``the agent
suffered less adverse selection'' separately measurable rather than rhetorical.

An episode runs for $T$ with $n = T/\dt$ steps and inventory clipped to
$|q| \le Q$. The agent's objective is $\mathbb{E}\big[\sum_t r_t\big]$, undiscounted.

\section{The null world and its closed form}

At $\thnull$ market orders arrive on each side as a Poisson process of intensity
$\lambda$, a quote at distance $\delta$ is filled with probability
$e^{-\kappa\delta}$, and the mid is a driftless geometric Brownian motion. Cartea
and Jaimungal solve this exactly. Writing $h_q(t)$ for the value function's
inventory term, the optimal half-spreads are

\begin{equation}
  \delta^{*\pm} \;=\; \frac{1}{\kappa} \;+\; h_q \;-\; h_{q\mp1},
  \qquad
  \omega = e^{\kappa h} \text{ solves } \omega' = A\omega,
  \quad
  A_{q,q} = \kappa\phi q^2,
  \quad
  A_{q,q\pm1} = -\lambda/e .
\end{equation}

Two properties of this solution drive the whole design. It is \textbf{one-sided
symmetric}: the skew depends on $q$ and on time remaining, never on anything the
market is doing. And $\sigma$ does not appear in it at all --- the carry term
$q\,\mathrm{d}S$ is a martingale, so volatility never enters the Hamilton--Jacobi
equation and inventory risk is carried entirely by $\phi$.

\paragraph{Geometric mid.} The log mid evolves as
$\mathrm{d}\log S = (\alpha - \tfrac12\sigma^2)\mathrm{d}t + \sigma\,\mathrm{d}W$.
The It\^o term is what keeps $\mathbb{E}[\mathrm{d}S] = 0$ when $\alpha = 0$; without
it the mid drifts up and a long maker earns a spurious carry. Because $\sigma$
multiplies the \emph{log} increment it is a return per unit $\sqrt{\text{time}}$,
dimensionless, while $\kappa$ is in $1/\text{price}$ and $\delta$ is a price. The
price level $S_0$ therefore carries the scale, and the exact invariance is
\begin{equation}
  (S_0,\ \kappa,\ \phi) \;\longmapsto\; (aS_0,\ \kappa/a,\ a\phi),
  \qquad \sigma,\ \varepsilon,\ \zeta_{\mathrm{mkt}} \text{ unchanged},
\end{equation}
under which the criterion scales by $a$ and every ratio is unchanged. This is
verified numerically over a $625\times$ range in $S_0$.

Two diagnostics gate every world. \emph{Economic coherence}
$\sigma S_0 \mathbb{E}[\kappa]\sqrt{e/\mathbb{E}[\lambda]}$ compares the half-spread
earned per round trip against the price move suffered while holding; it is
expected to be of order 1, and far above it abstaining wins. \emph{Step
resolution} $\sigma S_0\sqrt{\dt}\,\vartheta$ is the fraction of a half-spread the
price covers in one step, and must stay small or the discretisation, not the
economics, sets the answer.

\section{Simulator layers}

Each layer adds one mechanism and can be switched off, which is what makes
$\thnull$ reachable exactly rather than approximately.

\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}clll@{}}
\toprule
layer & object & off at $\thnull$ when & mechanism \\
\midrule
1 & mid price & --- & geometric Brownian motion \\
2 & short-term drift & $\varepsilon=\rho=\zeta_{\mathrm{mkt}}=0$ & flow-driven $\alpha$, transient and permanent \\
3 & order flow & $\eta=\nu=0$ & mutually exciting Hawkes intensities \\
4 & book depth & $\eta_\kappa=\nu_\kappa=0$ & flow-driven $\kappa^\pm$ \\
5 & volatility & $\sigma_\sigma=0$ & log-OU stochastic volatility \\
6 & own impact & $\gamma_{\mathrm{self}}=\zeta_{\mathrm{perm}}=0$ & our own fills move depth and mid \\
7 & execution & --- & coupled thinning of the same market orders \\
8 & environment & --- & observation, reward, episode \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

Layer 7 is the one that makes adverse selection exist. A market order is drawn
once and then drives \emph{both} the fill indicator
$F^{\pm} = \mathbb{1}\{\mathrm{Bin}(n^{\pm}, e^{-\kappa\delta}) \ge 1\}$ and the jump
in $\alpha$. Drawing them separately would leave a world where getting filled
tells the maker nothing, and every result about information would be an artefact.

\section{Relaxation axes}

One coefficient at a time, from $\thnull$ outwards. Each axis names the
observation block that could in principle carry its information.

\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}llll@{}}
\toprule
axis & coefficients & mechanism & block \\
\midrule
1 & $\sigma_\sigma$ & stochastic volatility & regime \\
2 & $\eta,\nu$ & flow clustering & regime \\
3 & $\varepsilon,\rho$ & adverse selection, transient & signal \\
3d & $\zeta_{\mathrm{mkt}},\rho$ & adverse selection, permanent & signal \\
4 & $\eta_\kappa,\nu_\kappa$ & reactive depth & book \\
5 & $k_{\mathrm{lat}}$ & quote latency (a \texttt{Config} choice) & --- \\
6 & $\gamma_{\mathrm{self}}$ & own-order impact & book \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

Axes 1 and 2 must show the closed form \emph{holding}. Its solution does not use
$\sigma$, and it uses $\lambda$ only through its level, so stochastic volatility
and mean-preserving clustering should cost it little. If it collapses there, that
is an implementation defect and not a discovery --- which is why those two axes
are run first and also serve as negative controls for the bias correction.

\section{What makes a measured gain credible}

The protocol is identical on every axis, and each item exists to kill one specific
alternative explanation.

\begin{enumerate}[label=\arabic*.]
\item \textbf{The world is checked before the agents run.} Measured moments ---
  $\mathbb{E}[\lambda]$, $\mathbb{E}[\kappa]$, the intensity autocorrelation, the
  markout curve --- against their analytical targets. An axis whose moments are
  wrong tests nothing.
\item \textbf{Common random numbers.} Every policy in a $(\phi,\text{seed})$ cell
  sees the same drawn world and the same market orders, so all comparisons are
  paired. The script verifies this itself: baseline criteria must be identical to
  the bit across observation layouts, or the observation is perturbing the world.
\item \textbf{Three closed forms, not one.} At the true $\bar\lambda$, at the
  stationary $\mathbb{E}[\lambda]$, and refitted on a rolling window. The first gap
  isolates a \emph{level} error, which a practitioner would simply re-estimate
  away; what remains is blindness to the \emph{state}, and only that part is
  available to an agent.
\item \textbf{A blind agent of the same size and budget.} ``The agent beats the
  closed form'' proves nothing if an agent seeing only $(q,t)$ beats it too, since
  it can gain by adapting to the inventory bound rather than to the regime. The
  reported quantity is the informed-minus-blind gap.
\item \textbf{Two budgets.} Adding an observation block raises the parameter count
  and degrades the optimisation, so at equal budget an informed agent can lose for
  a reason unrelated to information. Each axis is run at two budgets and the
  gradient quality is reported alongside.
\item \textbf{Baselines other than the closed form.} A random quoter and fixed
  half-spreads at $0.5/\kappa$, $1/\kappa$ and $2/\kappa$, so the reader can see
  what the problem is worth before any optimisation.
\end{enumerate}

Results are reported in financial units --- basis points of notional, basis points
per fill, Sharpe ratio per episode and annualised, maximum drawdown, hit rate ---
alongside the raw criterion, so that the size of a gain is legible without knowing
the model's internal scale.

\section{Deliverables}

\begin{enumerate}[label=\arabic*.]
\item A layered simulator with $\thnull$ reachable exactly, every layer separately
  switchable, and the reward identity asserted at each step.
\item An independent implementation of the Cartea--Jaimungal solution, validated
  at $\thnull$ against a policy search over the same family.
\item Two learners --- PPO and an evolution strategy --- sharing the environment,
  the action space and the evaluation.
\item Per-axis measurement tables, with the negative controls and the bias
  correction they imply.
\item A calibration against public Binance data, stating which coefficients it
  identifies and which it cannot.
\item Figures, a notebook and an interactive dashboard reproducing the reported
  numbers.
\end{enumerate}
